# Space Economy Thesis

## Thesis

Space is transitioning from government monopoly to commercial industry. Declining launch costs (reusable rockets) are unlocking new mission categories — mega-constellations, direct-to-device satellite connectivity, lunar infrastructure, and defense space systems. The sector resembles early aviation: massive capex, high failure rates, but the structural demand shift is real and multi-decade.

## Variant View

- **Market view**: public space equities are expensive, failure-prone, and structurally disadvantaged by SpaceX.
- **My view**: selected non-SpaceX niches can still compound if they control scarce execution capability, sovereign contracts, or differentiated mission infrastructure.
- **Why this matters**: the thesis should scale through RKLB/MDA-style execution evidence, while binary names like LUNR/ASTS stay capped until milestones convert into revenue.

## Market Size

> [!analysis] I am not anchoring this thesis on a single space-economy TAM forecast.
> Space Foundation, Morgan Stanley, BofA, and other market-size studies use materially different definitions and time horizons, so the investable signal here is launch cadence, defense demand, backlog quality, and funding durability rather than any one headline number.

## Secular Tailwinds

1. **Declining launch costs** — reusable rockets compress per-kg pricing; annual objects launched from ~130 (1990s) to 4,500+ (2025)
2. **Mega-constellation deployment** — Starlink, Amazon Kuiper, and Telesat Lightspeed are driving satellite manufacturing and launch demand
3. **Defense/sovereignty** — US SDA programs, Canadian sovereign-space procurement, and European rearmament are making defense a leading demand driver
4. **Direct-to-device connectivity** — 3GPP NTN standards formalized satellite-5G integration; ASTS, Starlink Direct-to-Cell, Apple Emergency SOS
5. **Lunar economy** — Artemis program (crewed mission launched Apr 2026), NASA CLPS program

## Major Risks

- **SpaceX competitive overhang** — dominant in launch, satellite internet, potential IPO crowds out competitors
- **Extreme capital intensity** — most companies burn hundreds of millions before profitability
- **Technical execution** — rockets fail, satellites malfunction, constellations take years longer than planned
- **Valuation disconnect** — many space stocks trade at 50-500x revenue; steep drawdowns on missed milestones
- **Government budget/policy risk** — Artemis changes flagged by MDA; admin priorities shift

## Key Position Details

**RKLB**: The highest-conviction name in the theme because it already has operational launch history and a credible path to a larger systems business. Electron is the base; Neutron is the growth option. The main risk is valuation, not whether the company has real execution capability.

**LUNR**: The lunar-infrastructure bet is compelling, but mission execution remains binary. A successful IM-3 materially improves credibility; another failure would damage the thesis fast even if NASA keeps funding multiple providers.

**ASTS**: The direct-to-device thesis is real, but this remains the highest speculation-to-revenue ratio in the portfolio. Commercial service and launch execution need to be close to flawless for the current valuation to hold up.

**MDA.TO**: The quality name in the theme. It is already profitable, tied into sovereign programs, and less dependent on blue-sky capital markets than the more speculative names. Conviction is capped by account constraints, not by business quality.

## Updates

### 2026-04-19 — BlueBird 7 launch failure (H3 signal)
ASTS BlueBird 7 launched on Blue Origin New Glenn (third flight, first booster reuse). New Glenn upper stage malfunction placed satellite into orbit too low to sustain operations (~154 km perigee vs. 460 km target). AST confirmed satellite will de-orbit; expects insurance recovery.

Negative signal for H3 (commercial service by end of 2026) but does not confirm or disconfirm — AST still targets 3 more BlueBirds ready to ship in ~30 days and ~45 in orbit by year-end. Competitive pressure is real: Starlink Direct-to-Cell has 10M+ active users. At 488x EV/Revenue, execution misses like this are punishing. No position change warranted yet — watch next launch and constellation cadence.
