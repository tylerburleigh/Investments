# Tech Conviction Thesis

## Thesis

These are quality compounders held for long-term wealth building. The common thread: dominant platform positions with durable competitive moats, compounding revenue, and management teams that allocate capital well. We'd hold these through normal market cycles.

Separate from the [[strategy/thesis-ai|AI thesis]] positions, which are speculative infrastructure bets on the AI capex cycle. These companies benefit from AI, but we'd own them regardless.

**Caveat**: A serious AI demand collapse (not normal volatility — a structural repricing of AI capex expectations) could justify cutting positions in NVDA, META, and GOOG, which derive significant revenue from the AI cycle. MSFT is the most defensive. This isn't the base case, but it's a tail risk worth acknowledging.

## Variant View

- **Market view**: mega-cap tech is well-covered and rarely obviously cheap; AI enthusiasm can make quality look safer than it is.
- **My view**: these are not thematic lottery tickets; they are durable platform compounders that should survive normal cycles and earn larger sizing than speculative themes.
- **Why this matters**: position sizing should be governed by moat durability, free cash flow, regulation, and capital allocation rather than short-term AI narrative strength.

## Market Size

> [!analysis] I am not anchoring this thesis on any single cloud, accelerator, digital-ad, or foundry TAM.
> These holdings earn their place through company-level moats, cash generation, and capital allocation. Top-down market-size estimates vary materially by definition and are not the deciding evidence here.

## Secular Tailwinds

1. **AI monetization compounding** — NVDA (chips), META (ad targeting), GOOG (search + cloud), MSFT (Copilot) all converting AI capex into revenue
2. **Enterprise cloud adoption** — Azure, AWS, GCP still growing 20%+ as workloads migrate
3. **Platform lock-in** — CUDA ecosystem (NVDA), Office/Azure (MSFT), search/ad network (GOOG), social graph (META) create durable moats
4. **Semiconductor demand** — AI, automotive, IoT driving structural demand growth; TSM benefits as sole advanced-node foundry
5. **Capital allocation discipline** — all six companies generate massive free cash flow and return capital via buybacks/dividends

## Major Risks

- **Antitrust / regulatory** — GOOG DOJ case ongoing; META facing EU DMA enforcement; antitrust overhang on all dominant platforms
- **AI bubble contagion** — a serious AI capex pullback (see caveat above) would hit NVDA first, then META/GOOG via ad spend contraction
- **Geopolitical (TSM)** — Taiwan Strait risk is existential for TSM; no hedge beyond position sizing
- **Competitive erosion (AMD)** — AMD is the challenger in both GPU and data center CPU; MI300 gaining share but execution risk is higher than NVDA
- **Valuation stretch** — NVDA trades at premium multiples; any growth deceleration triggers sharp repricing

## Key Position Details

**NVDA**: The highest-growth name in the basket and the one most exposed to the AI capex cycle. CUDA and platform cadence remain the moat; valuation is the risk.

**META**: Core thesis is the durability of the social graph plus the ability to turn AI investment into better ad monetization. The valuation is not the issue; regulation and capital-allocation discipline are.

**GOOG**: Search remains the cash engine, Cloud is the diversification lever, and DeepMind is the strategic AI asset. The main risk remains regulation, not business quality.

**MSFT**: The most defensive name in the set because the recurring enterprise base matters more than near-term AI excitement. Copilot is upside, not the entire thesis.

**AMD**: Strong second-place exposure in two large markets. The thesis is not "beat NVIDIA" but "take enough share in CPUs and accelerators to compound from a lower base."

**TSM**: The quality is obvious; the question is how much geopolitical risk should live inside position sizing. Without Taiwan risk this would likely screen as the strongest business in the basket.

## Allocation / Sizing

Target: 10% of portfolio. Current: 7.7% — underweight. Daily DCA in non-registered account.

**Priority for new capital**: MSFT and NVDA first (highest conviction, underweight). META and GOOG second. AMD and TSM third — already at conviction 3 and smaller positions are appropriate given their respective risks.

- **Operating band**: 7-13% of portfolio
- **Max thesis weight**: 15% without a strategy-level allocation update
- **Max single-name weight**: 5% default review threshold for individual equities
- **Conviction 4 posture**: allow MSFT/NVDA/META/GOOG to compound through normal volatility, but review any one name above 5%
- **Conviction 3 posture**: AMD and TSM stay smaller unless a specific thesis changes; TSM geopolitical risk caps sizing despite business quality
- **DCA posture**: continue toward 10% while thesis intact; pause if above 13% or if AI-demand/regulatory tail risks become active

- **Company fundamentals** — revenue growth, margins, free cash flow, and buyback discipline
- **AI monetization** — NVDA data center demand, META/GOOG ad lift, MSFT Copilot/Azure contribution
- **Regulatory risk** — DOJ/FTC/EU actions affecting GOOG and META operations or structure
- **Competitive risk** — AMD accelerator share, CUDA switching evidence, and cloud workload migration
- **TSM roadmap / geopolitics** — N2/A16 execution, customer concentration, and Taiwan Strait escalation
- **Valuation discipline** — multiple expansion unsupported by earnings growth, especially in NVDA
