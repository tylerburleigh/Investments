# EV / Electric Transportation Theme

> Theme within: [[strategy/thesis-energy-transition]]

## Thesis

Transportation electrification is a multi-decade structural shift. This theme takes exposure through Chinese EV manufacturers — the largest and most competitive EV market globally. Two complementary approaches: **NIO** (premium, battery swap, BEV-only) and **LI** (family SUVs, EREV + BEV, fortress balance sheet). Both are venture-scale bets on surviving a brutal consolidation — most Chinese EV startups won't make it independently. The survivors will emerge with massive domestic scale and export optionality.

## Variant View

- **Market view**: Chinese EV competition is too brutal and margins too thin for most public equity exposure to be attractive.
- **My view**: the only investable angle is survivor optionality: own very small positions in companies with differentiated products, enough liquidity, and plausible paths back to margin.
- **Why this matters**: this theme should stay venture-scale unless deliveries, margin, and balance-sheet evidence improve together.

## Market Size

> [!analysis] I am not anchoring this theme on a single global EV market-size forecast.
> Vehicle-only, battery-only, and infrastructure-inclusive estimates vary too widely across vendors to treat any one TAM as ground truth.

> [!source] IEA Global EV Outlook 2025 (2025): electric car sales exceeded 17 million globally in 2024, more than 20% of new car sales, and China remained the largest EV market.
> https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2025/executive-summary

## Secular Tailwinds

1. **Regulatory mandates** — EU 2035 ICE ban, California/ZEV state mandates, China NEV policies
2. **Battery cost declines** — lithium-ion costs have fallen dramatically over the past decade, improving EV economics even if an exact parity threshold is hard to pin down
3. **Model expansion** — every major OEM launching EV models across all segments
4. **Charging infrastructure buildout** — government and private investment scaling rapidly
5. **China EV dominance** — Chinese EV makers producing compelling vehicles at lower cost than Western peers

## Major Risks

- **Intense competition** — BYD, Tesla, and dozens of Chinese startups fighting for share; margin pressure everywhere
- **Policy reversal** — EV mandates could be softened or delayed; subsidies being phased out
- **Battery supply chain risk** — lithium, cobalt, rare earth supply concentrated in a few countries
- **Trade barriers** — tariffs on Chinese EVs (US 100%, EU tariffs) could reshape the market
- **Cash burn** — most EV startups are unprofitable and dependent on capital markets

**NIO**: Chinese premium EV maker. **LI**: Li Auto is the balance-sheet-heavy counterweight to NIO. The attraction is not "best EV company in China" but "company with enough cash and product refresh catalysts to survive a difficult reset if execution improves." No position yet.
